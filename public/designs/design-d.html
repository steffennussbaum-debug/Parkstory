TilePark XY

Parking that organizes itself.

One new architecture for parking, built on three legacies of engineering, software and digital entrepreneurship. Here is how TilePark works, who we are, and why you can rely on it for decades.

How TilePark works

Not just a better machine. A different architecture.

Conventional automated garages move cars with a few central machines along fixed paths. TilePark turns the floor itself into the machine: hundreds of independent conveyors, no central control, one shared goal.

For developers and architects

A different architecture changes the economics of your building.

Every space in a conventional garage comes with its share of ramps and driving lanes. TilePark has neither. The floor you no longer give to cars can go back to the building: more apartments, more rentable space, less excavation.

How it works

A garage that works like a sliding puzzle.

In a conventional garage, roughly half of the floor area is taken up by ramps and driving lanes. TilePark needs neither. Each car rests on a pallet, and the conveyors beneath move it lengthwise and sideways, just like the pieces of a sliding puzzle. A single empty space is enough to bring any car to the transfer cabin.

The third dimension

Add Z. Up to four cars on the footprint of one.

The XY grid moves cars across the floor. Add a TilePark stacker above selected conveyors, or above every conveyor, and the footprint of a single car holds up to four cars instead of one, still without ramps, driving lanes, or a central lift tower.

XY handles the logistics

The transport level brings each car beneath its stacker, using the same sliding-puzzle routing.

The stacker lifts it up

The stacker raises the car into a storage level directly above its conveyor. The storage levels need no conveyors, because cars are stacked as in a silo.

And brings it back down

On request, the stacker lowers the car onto a free conveyor, which carries it to the transfer cabin.

Still no single point of failure

Each stacker serves only its own conveyor. With no central lift tower that every car depends on, the rest of the garage keeps running.

Why Z pays off

One automation level. Everything above is simple storage.

All of the automation is concentrated on the ground floor; only the transport level has conveyors. The levels above are stacked like a silo and need neither conveyors nor floor slabs. The building becomes simpler, lower, and less expensive, and its full height can be used for parking.

Conveyors on one level onlyConveyors and drives are installed on the transport level alone. The storage levels are simple steel shelving, which means less equipment to purchase and maintain.
No slabs to buildA stacked silo replaces intermediate parking decks, eliminating the cost of concrete slabs, formwork, and reinforcement.
No height lost to slabsWithout slabs and their build-up, every meter of height is used for cars, so more levels fit within the same building.
Lower build-up heightThe XY conveyor level itself is very low, which saves floor height, pit depth and construction cost.
Lightweight constructionThe light steel structure means lower loads on the building, smaller foundations, and an easy fit in existing structures.
Less excavationA lower structure for the same number of cars means a shallower pit, reducing the cost of excavation, shoring, and waterproofing.

It unlocks height. Whether in a basement, on a ground floor, or in a dedicated building, the clear height you already have becomes parking capacity.

The heart of TilePark

No central control.
No central point of failure.

Just like bees in a hive, each TilePark conveyor has its own controller and acts independently. There is no master computer, shuttle, or lift that every car depends on. This decentralized control is the core of our technology and the source of the system's speed, flexibility, and resilience.

Conveyors move in parallelMany conveyors move simultaneously, so no car has to wait for the one ahead of it.
A new route every timeEach sequence is calculated for the grid's current layout, so the system adapts rather than replaying a fixed program.
Robust by designA modular structure built from industry-standard components means fewer faults and fast replacement. Remote diagnostics let our team respond to any issue immediately.
A single fault never stops the garageIf a conveyor goes offline, the others route around it, just like bees in a hive. Try "Simulate conveyor fault" in the sliding-puzzle demo above.

And if the power goes out? Your car still comes out.

TilePark requires remarkably little power. With as little as 30 amps, the entire system can run on a generator, so drivers can still collect their cars during a power outage.

  • Low energy use. On the XY level, cars only ever move horizontally, along the shortest optimized routes. Only the transport level is automated, and only the conveyors needed for a sequence move.
  • Multiple redundancy. Every conveyor has at least four drive motors, and every conveyor can take over for any other.

Patented principle

The TilePark XY sliding-puzzle principle is protected by European patent EP 3924576 B1, with further patents pending in the US and Germany.

Integrated EV charging

Every space can charge. Every charger keeps working.

With fixed charging bays, a fully charged car blocks the charger until its driver returns. TilePark moves each EV to a charger, charges it to the level requested in the app, and then moves it away so the next vehicle can charge. Chargers stay in use all night, and far fewer are needed. An infrastructure decision

A garage is built once. You live with it for decades.

Once ramps, driving lanes and lift shafts are cast in concrete, the parking decision shapes the building for its entire life. The real question is not whether TilePark is better today. It is what the alternatives will cost you over the next 30 years: in floor area, in flexibility and in risk.

Conventional garage

  • Ramps and driving lanes take a large share of every level, for the life of the building.
  • More excavation, or more levels, for the same number of cars.
  • Sloped ramps and low ceilings make later conversion difficult and costly.

Other automated parking systems

  • A few central lifts or shuttles carry every car. They set the throughput, and when one stops, the cars behind it wait.
  • Racks and aisles fix the building grid and the capacity from day one.
  • Technology frozen at the state of the day it was chosen.

TilePark XY

  • No ramps, no driving lanes: more than 150 sq ft per car goes back to the building.
  • Hundreds of independent conveyors. If one is offline, the others route around it.
  • A modular grid that fits irregular sites, and software that keeps improving through updates.

Before you commit your building to a conventional layout or another automated parking system, find out what TilePark could change.

Inspired by nature

We asked the bees how to park.

Bees have been solving our problem for millions of years: storing as much as possible in as little space as possible, and getting everywhere fast. So we asked them how they do it. Here are their rules, and how TilePark follows each one.

The bees' rules for parking

  1. 1

    “Don't waste a single cell.”

    The beesA honeycomb stores the most in the least space. No other shape divides an area into equal cells with less wall, a fact proven mathematically in 1999.

    TileParkEvery position is a conveyor. No ramps, no driving lanes, no wasted floor, and the grid adapts to irregular plots and columns.

  2. 2

    “Take the shortest way, with the least effort.”

    The beesBees learn the shortest route between flowers, with a brain the size of a grass seed. Every flight costs honey, so they keep flying to a minimum.

    TileParkFor every request the system calculates the fastest route for the grid's current layout. Cars only move horizontally on the XY level, and the whole system can run on as little as 30 amps.

  3. 3

    “Nobody is in charge. If a cell breaks, go around it.”

    The beesA hive has no boss and no master plan. Bees simply work around a damaged cell, and the colony keeps going.

    TileParkEach conveyor has its own controller and hands cars to its neighbors. If one goes offline, the others route around it and every car stays accessible.

Built for artificial intelligence

Swarm intelligence, made for machine learning.

Because TilePark works like a swarm, with many independent conveyors and countless possible routes, it is an ideal foundation for artificial intelligence. Every sequence the system runs is a lesson in which routes are fastest and which storage positions work best. The more it runs, the smarter it gets.

Every garage contributes to this shared intelligence through anonymous usage patterns, such as typical arrival and departure times, never through personal tracking. TilePark has already begun implementing these learning capabilities.

Quality control

Every conveyor segment is tested under load.

Before a conveyor leaves our factory, it goes onto the test rig. A hydraulic press applies load from above while the drives run, just as they will under a parked car. Only conveyors that pass are released for shipping.

  • Drives, gearboxes and rollers run under load, not just switched on.
  • Structure and alignment are checked while the conveyor carries weight.
  • Every result is logged against the component's serial number.
